# RedNote Content Style Analysis Framework

Apply this to each post pulled by the scraper, starting with your top 15-20
by engagement score. Goal: reverse-engineer the *pattern*, not copy specific
posts.

## 1. Hook (first line / title)
- What's the opening line or title doing? Common patterns: a question, a
  bold claim, a number/list promise, a relatable complaint, a "POV" framing.
- Does it front-load curiosity (withholds the payoff) or front-load value
  (states the benefit immediately)?

## 2. Keypoints
- What 2-4 concrete facts/details does the post actually convey about the
  product or place (price, ingredient, origin story, limited availability,
  a specific dish)?
- Are these keypoints stated directly, or revealed through a narrative
  ("I tried X and discovered Y")?

## 3. Selling points
- Which selling point is foregrounded: taste, price/value, nostalgia,
  authenticity/heritage, novelty, convenience, social currency ("good for
  photos / good to recommend to friends")?
- Is there a single dominant selling point per post, or several stacked?

## 4. Sales psychology techniques present
Check which of these show up, and how:
- Scarcity / urgency (limited time, limited stock, seasonal)
- Social proof (queue photos, "everyone's talking about", review screenshots)
- Authority (chef's background, heritage/origin claims, awards/certifications)
- Reciprocity (giving a tip/guide "for free" before any mention of the brand)
- Nostalgia / identity (childhood memory, "this is what real X tastes like")
- Problem-solution (a complaint about competitors/own past experience, then
  resolved by this product)
- Curiosity gap (title withholds the reveal)

## 5. Why it doesn't read as an ad
- First-person, casual tone vs brand-voice tone?
- Framed as personal discovery/recommendation rather than promotion?
- Imperfections included (casual photos, mentions of a minor downside) to
  read as authentic UGC?
- Native platform formatting (emoji density, line breaks, hashtag style)
  matching how regular users post, not how brands post elsewhere?

## 6. Virality triggers
- Is there a "save-worthy" practical hook (a guide, a list, a comparison)
  that explains the high collect count?
- Any controversy, comparison, or strong opinion that invites comments?
- Local/seasonal trend tie-in (a moment, a season, a meme format)?
- Visual hook in the cover image (text overlay, before/after, food close-up)?

## 7. Scoring (optional, for comparing posts at a glance)
Rate each post 1-5 on: hook strength, keypoint clarity, psychology technique
density, ad-disguise quality, virality trigger count. Posts that score high
across most categories are your best templates to study structurally --
not to copy text from, but to model the *shape* of (hook style, pacing,
selling point placement) for your own ipohtown.asia content.
